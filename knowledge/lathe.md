## 1. Machine and parts

### Lathe in general
- [LAT-F001] A lathe is a machine tool employed generally to produce cylindrical objects. [HO p.1]
- [LAT-F002] The lathe is called the "mother of all machine tools". [HO p.1]
- [LAT-F003] Turning on a lathe removes excess material from the workpiece to produce a cone-shaped (tapered) or cylindrical surface. [HO p.1]
- [LAT-F004] Types of lathe listed in the handout: engine lathe, speed lathe, turret lathe, capstan lathe and CNC lathe. [HO p.1]
- [LAT-F005] The engine (centre) lathe is the most commonly used general-purpose lathe in engineering workshops. [HO p.2]
- [LAT-F006] Working principle of the lathe: the workpiece rotates (rotary motion of the work) while a single-point cutting tool is given a feed motion, producing a turned surface. [HO p.2]
- [LAT-F007] In a lathe the workpiece rotates and the tool is fed; in milling and grinding it is the tool (cutter/wheel) that rotates. [NB p.11–12]
- [LAT-F008] There must always be relative motion between the tool and the workpiece for cutting to occur. [NB p.11]
- [LAT-F009] Centre-lathe turning is a metal-removal (machining) process. [NB p.11]

### Main parts and their functions
- [LAT-F010] The main parts of a centre lathe are the headstock, bed, tailstock, carriage (saddle, cross-slide, compound rest, tool post, apron), lead screw, feed rod, chuck, main spindle and legs. [LEC s.7]
- [LAT-F011] The bed is the main (principal) supporting structure of a lathe; the headstock, tailstock and carriage are all mounted on it. [HO p.3; LEC s.13]
- [LAT-F012] The tailstock and the carriage slide over the guideways (bedways) provided on the top face of the bed. [HO p.3]
- [LAT-F013] The lathe bed is generally made of grey cast iron because of its good damping, rigidity and self-lubricating qualities. [LEC s.13]
- [LAT-F014] The bed material should have high compressive strength and high wear resistance; cast iron alloyed with nickel and chromium is a good bed material. [HO p.3]
- [LAT-F015] Lathe beds have two types of guideways: inner ways and outer ways. [LEC s.13]
- [LAT-F016] The headstock is mounted on the left-hand side of the lathe bed. [HO p.3]
- [LAT-F017] The headstock houses the main spindle, the speed-change mechanism (gear box) and the change gears. [LEC s.14]
- [LAT-F018] The headstock gear box helps vary the spindle speed and also transmits power to the feed rod and lead screw. [HO p.3]
- [LAT-F019] The chuck or face plate is attached to the headstock spindle, which grips and rotates the workpiece. [HO p.3; LEC s.14]
- [LAT-F020] The headstock spindle carries the live centre, which rotates with the work. [LEC s.14; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F021] The tailstock is mounted on the right-hand side of the lathe bed. [HO p.4]
- [LAT-F022] The tailstock supports the free end of the workpiece and holds tools such as drills, reamers, boring and tapping tools. [HO p.4; LEC s.15]
- [LAT-F023] The tailstock can be moved along the bed guideways to accommodate workpieces of different lengths. [HO p.4]
- [LAT-F024] The tailstock carries the dead centre, which does not rotate. [LEC s.15; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F025] Tailstock parts named in the lecture: feed screw (handwheel), reduction gear box (optional), body, adjustable base, spindle (quill) and locking lever. [LEC s.15]
- [LAT-F026] The carriage slides on the bed guideways and carries the saddle, cross-slide, compound rest and tool post; it supports, moves and controls the cutting tool. [HO p.4; LEC s.16]
- [LAT-F027] The saddle is an H-shaped casting that fits over the bed and slides along the guideways, carrying the cross-slide and tool post; it can be locked in any position. [LEC s.17]
- [LAT-F028] Movement of the saddle parallel to the lathe axis gives the (longitudinal) feed. [HO p.5]
- [LAT-F029] The cross-slide is mounted on top of the saddle and moves the tool perpendicular (at right angles) to the lathe axis. [HO p.5]
- [LAT-F030] The cross-slide is moved by the cross-slide handwheel; the perpendicular distance it moves sets the depth of cut. [HO p.5]
- [LAT-F031] The compound rest (compound slide / top slide) is mounted on top of the cross-slide. [HO p.6]
- [LAT-F032] The compound rest has a circular base graduated in degrees and is used to obtain angular cuts and short tapers. [LEC s.19; HO p.6]
- [LAT-F033] In taper turning by the compound-rest method, the compound-slide handwheel is used to give the feed (by hand). [LEC s.19]
- [LAT-F034] The tool post is mounted on top of the compound rest and holds the cutting tool rigidly. [HO p.6; LEC s.20]
- [LAT-F035] Types of tool post: single-screw tool post, four-way tool post and eight-way tool post. [LEC s.20]
- [LAT-F036] The apron hangs in front of the carriage (saddle) and contains the carriage handwheel, half-nut lever and feed lever. [LEC s.16; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F037] The feed rod is a long shaft extending from the feed box; it moves the carriage or cross-slide for turning, boring and facing. [HO p.7]
- [LAT-F038] The lead screw is a long threaded shaft driven from the headstock and is used only for thread cutting; it is disengaged during normal turning. [HO p.7]
- [LAT-F039] The half-nut mechanism engages the carriage with the lead screw for thread cutting. [LEC s.22]
- [LAT-F040] The chip tray (chip pan) under the bed collects chips; the legs support the whole machine. [NB p.12; LEC s.12]
- [LAT-F041] Headstock controls named in the lecture include a high–low gear lever, tumbler gears (forward/reverse feed), a quick-change gear box and a forward–reverse switch. [LEC s.14]

### Feed mechanism
- [LAT-F042] A lathe tool has three types of feed: longitudinal (parallel to the lathe axis, by carriage movement), cross (at right angles to the axis) and angular (by swivelling the compound slide). [LEC s.21]
- [LAT-F043] Longitudinal and cross feeds can be given by hand or by power, but angular feed is given only by hand. [LEC s.21]

### Specification of a lathe
- [LAT-F044] A lathe is specified by: length of bed, distance between centres, swing over bed, swing over carriage, power, lead-screw details (pitch), number of spindle speeds, feeds and width of bed. [HO p.13; LEC s.7]
- [LAT-F045] "Swing" is the largest work diameter that can be rotated over the lathe bed. [LEC s.7]

## 2. Work-holding devices
- [LAT-F046] Work-holding methods on a lathe: between centres driven by a carrier (dog) and catch (driving) plate; on a mandrel between centres; in a chuck; on a face plate or angle plate; in a collet. [HO p.8; LEC s.23–26]
- [LAT-F047] When work is held between centres, a lathe dog (carrier) is clamped to the work and is driven by the dog (catch) plate on the spindle. [LEC s.23]
- [LAT-F048] In a 3-jaw chuck all three jaws move together (simultaneously), so the work is self-centred; it holds cylindrical and hexagonal work. [LEC s.24; NB p.12]
- [LAT-F049] In a 4-jaw chuck each jaw moves independently, so it can hold work of irregular shapes and sizes but the work must be centred manually. [LEC s.24; NB p.12]
- [LAT-F050] The jaws of a 4-jaw chuck are reversible. [LEC s.24]
- [LAT-F051] A 4-jaw (independent) chuck grips more firmly than a 3-jaw chuck, while a 3-jaw chuck is quicker to set because it centres automatically. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F052] A collet has three slits so that it can be squeezed onto bar stock by a sleeve. [LEC s.25]
- [LAT-F053] A face plate is attached to the spindle and work is clamped to it (e.g. with 4 clamps); used for irregular work that a chuck cannot hold. [LEC s.26]
- [LAT-F054] The chuck jaws are tightened and loosened with a chuck key. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 4. Workpiece and tool material
- [LAT-F060] The workpiece in the ME-106 lathe job is low-carbon (mild) steel. [NB p.11]
- [LAT-F061] Mild (low-carbon) steel contains less than about 0.3% carbon. [NB p.16; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F062] The cutting tool used on the lathe is made of high-speed steel (HSS). [NB p.11, p.17]
- [LAT-F063] The tool must always be harder than the workpiece material. [NB p.11]
- [LAT-F064] The common 18-4-1 HSS contains about 18% tungsten, 4% chromium and 1% vanadium. [NB p.17; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F065] HSS is called "high speed" steel because it retains its hardness (red hardness) at the high temperatures of high-speed machining. [NB p.17]
- [LAT-F066] Medium-carbon steel contains about 0.3–0.6% C and high-carbon steel about 0.6–1.5% C. [theory: Hajra Choudhury] (corrects notebook — see corrections.md)
- [LAT-F067] Steel contains up to about 2.1% carbon; above this the iron–carbon alloy is cast iron, and 6.67% C corresponds to cementite (Fe₃C). [theory: Callister] (corrects notebook)

## 5. Single-point cutting tool
- [LAT-F068] Parts of a single-point cutting tool: shank, face, flank, heel, base, nose and point (cutting edge). [HO p.8]
- [LAT-F069] The shank is the body of the tool held in the tool post; the face is the surface over which the chip flows; the flank is the surface below the cutting edge facing the work; the nose is the corner where side and end cutting edges meet. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F070] Rake angle is the angle of the tool face; it lets the chip flow away easily and reduces cutting force. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F071] Clearance (relief) angle is the angle of the flank that prevents the flank from rubbing against the work. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 6. Lathe operations (theory)
- [LAT-F072] Lathe operations listed: facing, straight turning, step turning, chamfering, knurling, grooving, parting-off, drilling, boring, reaming, taper turning, thread cutting, form turning. [HO p.9; LEC s.27; NB p.13–17]
- [LAT-F073] Facing produces a flat surface at the end of the workpiece and reduces its length; the tool is fed perpendicular to the lathe axis. [HO p.9; NB p.13]
- [LAT-F074] Straight (plain) turning reduces the diameter of the workpiece along its whole length; the tool is fed parallel to the lathe axis. [HO p.10; NB p.13]
- [LAT-F075] Step turning reduces the diameter over part of the length, producing steps (shoulders) of different diameters. [NB p.13]
- [LAT-F076] Chamfering bevels (slopes) the sharp end edge of the work to remove burrs and sharp edges after turning. [HO p.10; NB p.16]
- [LAT-F077] Knurling embosses a diamond or straight pattern on the surface (for grip) by pressing a knurling tool against the rotating work. [HO p.11]
- [LAT-F078] Grooving cuts a narrow groove (e.g. at the end of a thread) using a grooving tool shaped to the groove. [NB p.14]
- [LAT-F079] Parting-off cuts the finished job off from the bar; the carriage (longitudinal feed) is locked and the tool is fed in (plunged) radially until the work separates. [HO p.11; NB p.14]
- [LAT-F080] Centre drilling makes a conical centre hole at the end of the work so it can be supported between centres; a centre drill is used. [NB p.14]
- [LAT-F081] Taper turning produces a uniform decrease in diameter along the length; the amount of taper is given by the difference in diameters over the length. [HO p.12]
- [LAT-F082] Taper turning methods: compound-rest (swivelling) method, form-tool method, tailstock set-over method and taper-turning attachment. [LEC s.10, s.19; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F083] The compound-rest method is suited to short, steep tapers; the form-tool method to very short tapers; the tailstock set-over method to long, gentle tapers on work held between centres. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F084] Half taper angle: tan α = (D − d) / (2L), where D = large diameter, d = small diameter, L = length of taper. [HO p.12; LS p.1; NB p.14]
- [LAT-F085] Boring enlarges an existing hole; reaming finishes a drilled hole to size. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F086] Form turning uses a tool ground to the required profile, so the profile is reproduced on the work. [LEC s.9, s.27]

## 7. Thread cutting
- [LAT-F087] Thread cutting produces a helical groove on a cylindrical surface; any pitch, shape and size can be cut on a lathe using a single-point (V or square) tool. [HO p.12]
- [LAT-F088] For thread cutting, the tool is moved automatically along the work by the lead screw; the longitudinal feed per revolution of work must equal the pitch of the thread. [HO p.13; LEC s.22]
- [LAT-F089] Thread nomenclature: crest (top), root (bottom), flanks (sides), pitch (distance between adjacent threads), major diameter, minor (core) diameter and depth of thread. [NB p.15]
- [LAT-F090] A metric (ISO) V-thread has an included angle of 60°. [NB p.15]
- [LAT-F091] In the designation M20 × 1.5, "M" means metric thread, 20 mm is the major (nominal) diameter and 1.5 mm is the pitch. [NB p.15]
- [LAT-F092] Depth of a metric thread (as used in our lab): d = 0.6134 × p. [NB p.15; LS p.2; CLLS p.2]
- [LAT-F093] Core (minor) diameter: Dc = Dm − 2d, where Dm = major diameter and d = depth of thread. [NB p.15; LS p.2; CLLS p.2]
- [LAT-F094] Square threads are used in load-lifting and power-transmitting devices such as screw jacks. [NB p.15]
- [LAT-F095] A buttress thread has one flank inclined (about 45°) and the other nearly perpendicular; it transmits heavy load in one direction only (e.g. vices). [NB p.16; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F096] Thread-cutting speed is much lower than turning speed (e.g. 90 rpm vs 315 rpm in our lab). [LS p.2]

## 8. Cutting parameters
- [LAT-F097] Cutting speed V = πDN / 1000 m/min, where D = work diameter in mm and N = spindle speed in rpm. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F098] Feed is the distance the tool advances per revolution of the work (mm/rev). [theory: Hajra Choudhury] (corrects notebook)
- [LAT-F099] Depth of cut in turning = (D − d) / 2, i.e. half the reduction in diameter. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F100] The least count of the cross-slide graduated dial on our lathe is 0.02 mm. [LS p.1]

## 10. Procedure (as done in our lab)
- [LAT-F114] Step 1: measure the length and diameter of the raw workpiece. [LS p.1; CLLS p.1; NB p.17]
- [LAT-F115] Step 2: mark 115 mm on the workpiece and fix it in the four-jaw chuck. [LS p.1; CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F116] Step 3: check the centring of the work and set the spindle speed (315 rpm for turning). [LS p.1; NB p.17]
- [LAT-F117] Step 4: perform facing to bring the job to the desired length. [LS p.1; CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F118] After facing, the smaller diameters are turned; the 2025 log sheet then lists grooving and taper turning in the marked areas (the 2022 sheet also had a plain-turning pass over the whole length first). [CLLS p.1; LS p.1–2]
- [LAT-F119] Then grooving (5 mm) and taper turning with the compound rest swivelled to the calculated half-taper angle. [LS p.2; CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F120] For threading, the speed is reduced (90 rpm), the change gears are arranged from the chart for 1.5 mm pitch, and the thread is cut over the marked length. [LS p.2; CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F121] Order of steps on the 2025 lathe log sheet: measure → mark 115 mm & fix in 4-jaw chuck → check centre & set rotation → facing → grooving and taper turning → threading (1.5 mm pitch over 25 mm). [CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F122] Centre drilling and chamfering are also done on the lathe job (both ends centre-drilled; ends chamfered). [CLLS p.1; BOARD]

## 11. Precautions (lathe shop)
- [LAT-F123] Always wear the workshop apron, tight-fitting clothes and shoes in the lathe shop. [LS p.1; CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F124] Handle heavy machines and tools with care. [LS p.1; CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F125] Remove chips in time (with a brush, not the hand) to avoid accidents. [LS p.1]
- [LAT-F126] Move the tailstock carefully. [LS p.1]
- [LAT-F127] Stand at a proper distance from the machine, as hot chips may fly off. [CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F128] Take measurements in proper lighting using inside and outside calipers. [CLLS p.1]
- [LAT-F129] Remove the chuck key from the chuck before switching on the lathe. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F130] Stop the machine before measuring the job or changing the speed/gears. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 12. Advantages, limitations, applications
- [LAT-F131] The centre lathe is versatile: one machine can face, turn, taper-turn, thread, drill, bore, knurl and part-off. [HO p.9; LEC s.27]
- [LAT-F132] A centre lathe is mainly suited to rotationally symmetric (cylindrical) parts such as shafts, bolts, pins and bushes; it is slow for mass production compared to capstan/turret/CNC lathes. [theory: P.N. Rao]


## 13. Standard lathe practice (setting up, cutting and safety)
- [LAT-F133] The tool tip must be set exactly at centre height (level with the lathe axis); if it is above or below, the effective rake and clearance change and a small pip is left at the centre when facing. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F134] Tool overhang from the tool post should be kept as short as possible so the tool is rigid and does not chatter. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F135] Work in a 4-jaw chuck is centred by rotating the chuck by hand and checking run-out with chalk or a surface gauge/dial indicator, adjusting opposite jaws in pairs. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F136] Rough cuts (larger depth, coarser feed) are taken first and a light finishing cut (small depth, fine feed) last. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F137] For thread cutting: set the gear box/change gears for the pitch, set the tool square to the work with a centre (thread) gauge, engage the half-nut, and take several light cuts, withdrawing the tool and returning the carriage after each pass. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F138] A centre gauge (thread gauge) is used to grind the threading tool to 60° and to set it square to the work. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F139] A finished thread is checked with a screw-pitch gauge or by trying a mating nut. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F140] Before switching on, the chuck should be turned once by hand to check that the job and chuck jaws clear the tool and carriage. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F141] Never touch the rotating job or chuck, and never try to stop the chuck with your hand. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F142] Never change speeds or gears while the lathe is running. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F143] The tailstock must be locked to the bed (and the quill locked) before drilling or supporting work. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F144] Clamp the tool firmly in the tool post and the job firmly in the chuck before starting. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F145] Wear safety goggles on the lathe to protect the eyes from flying chips. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [LAT-F146] Do not lean on the machine or keep tools on the bed ways or carriage while the machine runs. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

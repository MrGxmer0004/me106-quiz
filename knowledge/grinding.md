Source keys: `LS` = Log Sheets ME-106.pdf p.7–8 (grinding log sheet); `NB` = Manufacturing_Notes_2023.pdf; `CG` = Cylindrical grinding.pdf; `GL` = Grinding Lab.pdf; `GP` = Grinding pdf.pdf; `GPPT` = GrindingPPT.pptx; `GR` = Grinding Ratio.jpg (blackboard). `theory:` = Hajra Choudhury Vol. II; P.N. Rao; Kalpakjian.

## 1. Principle and definitions
- [GRD-F001] Grinding is a machining process in which a rotating abrasive wheel removes very small amounts of material from the workpiece. [GPPT s.5]
- [GRD-F002] The abrasive grains of the wheel act like thousands of tiny cutting tools (multi-point cutting). [GPPT s.5; NB p.5]
- [GRD-F003] Grinding is a finishing process (small material removal), whereas machining such as turning or milling is semi-finishing by bulk material removal. [NB p.5; GL p.7]
- [GRD-F004] Usual manufacturing sequence: machining (bulk removal) → heat treatment (if necessary) → grinding (finishing). [GL p.7]
- [GRD-F005] Grinding is used after other machining processes when high precision and a smooth surface finish are required. [GPPT s.5]
- [GRD-F006] Value-addition processes in manufacturing: forming, metal addition (welding), melting and solidification (casting), and metal removal (machining, grinding). [GL p.1; NB p.5]
- [GRD-F007] Metal-removal processes include turning, shaping, planing, drilling, milling, broaching, gear manufacturing, thread cutting and grinding. [GL p.2]
- [GRD-F008] In grinding, material is removed by small, extremely hard (> 2000 kg/mm²), brittle refractory particles; chips are very small and cutting speeds are high (usually 30 m/s or more). [CG p.1]
- [GRD-F009] Grinding is divided into two regimes: stock-removal grinding (SRG) and form-and-finish grinding (FFG). [CG p.1]
- [GRD-F010] In stock-removal grinding the aim is to remove material without regard to surface quality (e.g. abrasive cut-off, conditioning of slabs and billets); wheel wear is so rapid that dressing is unnecessary. [CG p.1–2]
- [GRD-F011] In form-and-finish grinding, form and finish are the main concern, and the wheel must be dressed periodically. [CG p.1]
- [GRD-F012] In vertical-spindle surface grinding, grinding takes place on the face of the wheel instead of the periphery. [CG p.2]

## 2. Grinding ratio and specific energy
- [GRD-F013] Grinding ratio G = volume of work material removed / volume of wheel wear. [NB p.6; GR; CG p.3]
- [GRD-F014] For surface grinding of a block, volume of work removed V_w = l × b × Δh (length × breadth × reduction in height). [GR]
- [GRD-F015] Volume of wheel wear V_s = (π/4) × d × (D_i² − D_f²), where d = wheel width, D_i = initial and D_f = final wheel diameter. [GR]
- [GRD-F016] A higher grinding ratio means less wheel wear per unit of metal removed. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [GRD-F017] Specific energy is the energy needed to remove a unit volume (1 mm³) of material. [NB p.6]
- [GRD-F018] Specific energy in grinding (about 15–50 J/mm³) is much higher than in conventional machining (about 2–5 J/mm³). [NB p.6]
- [GRD-F019] Grinding has high specific energy because of very small chips, negative rake of the grains, rubbing and ploughing; most of this energy becomes heat, so coolant is essential. [theory: Kalpakjian]

## 3. Grinding machines
- [GRD-F020] Conventional grinding machines are classified as surface, cylindrical and internal grinding machines (plus tool-and-cutter and centreless grinders). [GL p.3; NB p.7; CG p.3; GP p.2]
- [GRD-F021] A surface grinding machine produces (finishes) flat surfaces. [GP p.2–3; GPPT s.9]
- [GRD-F022] Surface grinders have either a reciprocating table or a rotating table, each with a horizontal or vertical wheel spindle. [GP p.3]
- [GRD-F023] In a surface grinder the horizontal table moves back and forth while the wheel above is moved down to set the depth of cut. [GPPT s.9]
- [GRD-F024] Parts of a surface grinder: wheel, wheel guard, wheel head, column, table (with magnetic chuck), saddle, cross-feed and table-traverse handwheels, base. [GL p.4; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F025] Surface-grinding applications: machine beds, flat tools, gauges and dies. [GPPT s.9]
- [GRD-F026] A cylindrical grinding machine finishes external cylindrical surfaces (straight, tapered, stepped or profiled). [CG p.3; GP p.4]
- [GRD-F027] In cylindrical grinding the work is held between centres or in a chuck; the wheel rotates at high speed while the work rotates slowly. [GPPT s.10; GP p.4]
- [GRD-F028] In cylindrical grinding the table carrying the headstock, tailstock and work can be reciprocated by a hydraulic drive. [GP p.4]
- [GRD-F029] Parts of a cylindrical grinder (notebook sketch): headstock (work head), workpiece, grinding wheel (wheel head), tailstock, upper table, lower table, adjustable table dogs, base. [NB p.10]
- [GRD-F030] The adjustable dogs on the table set the limits of the table's reciprocating traverse. [NB p.10; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F031] A plain centre-type cylindrical grinder is similar to a centre lathe; the work is between headstock and tailstock centres and the disc wheel grinds with its periphery. [CG p.4]
- [GRD-F032] Traverse grinding feeds the work past the wheel along its length; plunge grinding feeds the wheel straight in without traverse. [CG p.4]
- [GRD-F033] A universal cylindrical grinder allows swivelling of the table, headstock and wheel head, so any taper can be ground; it also has an internal grinding attachment. [CG p.4]
- [GRD-F034] Cylindrical-grinding applications: shafts, rollers, precision bearings. [GPPT s.10]
- [GRD-F035] Internal grinding finishes the inside diameter (bores) of cylindrical work. [GP p.2; GL p.5]
- [GRD-F036] In a centreless grinder the work is not held between centres; it rests on a work-rest blade between the grinding wheel and a regulating wheel. [CG p.5; GP p.5; GPPT s.11]
- [GRD-F037] The regulating wheel is rubber-bonded, rotates slower than the grinding wheel, and controls the rotation of the work; both wheels rotate in the same direction. [GP p.5; CG p.5]
- [GRD-F038] In through-feed centreless grinding, the regulating wheel is tilted at a small angle so the work feeds axially past the wheel. [GP p.5; CG p.5]
- [GRD-F039] Centreless grinding suits high-volume grinding of long slender rods, pins and bars (e.g. valve spools, piston pins). [GP p.2; GPPT s.11]
- [GRD-F040] A tool-and-cutter grinder sharpens and reconditions cutting tools such as drills, end mills, milling cutters and gear cutters. [GPPT s.12; CG p.3]
- [GRD-F041] A tool-post grinder is a self-powered wheel mounted on the lathe tool post, the lathe spindle rotating the work. [CG p.5]

## 4. The grinding wheel and its designation
- [GRD-F042] A grinding wheel consists of abrasive grains (which cut), a bond (which holds the grains and gives shape), and pores (which carry chips away and let coolant reach the zone). [GPPT s.13; NB p.6]
- [GRD-F043] Standard wheel marking order: abrasive type – grain size – grade – structure – bond type (plus optional manufacturer's prefix/suffix). [NB p.7; CG p.2; GP p.12; GL p.9]
- [GRD-F044] Example A 46 K 5 V: aluminium oxide abrasive, grain size 46 (medium), grade K (medium), structure 5 (dense), vitrified bond. [GP p.12]
- [GRD-F045] In a full marking such as 51 A 60 K 5 V 05, the first number is the manufacturer's abrasive symbol and the last is the manufacturer's record (identifier). [GL p.9]
- [GRD-F046] Grinding wheel size is specified as diameter × thickness × bore. [CG p.2]
- [GRD-F047] Abrasive symbols: A = aluminium oxide (alumina), C = silicon carbide, D = diamond; CBN is a separate superabrasive (symbol B/CB). [NB p.7; CG p.2; GP p.12] (corrects notebook)
- [GRD-F048] Aluminium oxide is the most widely used abrasive, used for steels, ferrous alloys and high-tensile materials. [CG p.3; GPPT s.14]
- [GRD-F049] Silicon carbide is used for cast iron, non-ferrous metals and non-metals (low-tensile, hard brittle materials). [CG p.3; GPPT s.14; NB p.7]
- [GRD-F050] Diamond is used for very hard materials such as ceramics and carbides, and for final polishing (high hardness, high cost). [CG p.3]
- [GRD-F051] Cubic boron nitride (CBN) is used for very hard materials such as hardened tool steels. [GP p.7]
- [GRD-F052] Grain (grit) size number = number of openings per linear inch of the sieve used to grade the abrasive; a larger number means a finer grain. [GP p.8]
- [GRD-F053] Grain size classes (notebook): about 10–30 coarse, 30–80 medium, 80–200 fine, 200–600 very fine. [NB p.7; GP p.8; GPPT s.14]
- [GRD-F054] Fine grains are used for precision (finish) grinding; coarse/medium grains for rough grinding and fast stock removal. [GP p.8]
- [GRD-F055] Grade of a wheel = the strength (hardness) of the bond holding the grains, i.e. how easily grains are released; letters A to Z. [GP p.9; NB p.7; GPPT s.14]
- [GRD-F056] Grade letters: A–H soft, I–P medium, Q–Z hard. [NB p.7; GP p.9; GPPT s.14]
- [GRD-F057] A soft-grade wheel is used for grinding hard materials and a hard-grade wheel for soft materials. [NB p.7; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F058] Reason: on hard work grains blunt quickly, so a soft bond lets them break out and expose fresh sharp grains; on soft work grains stay sharp longer, so a hard bond keeps them in place. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F059] Grade selection depends on hardness of the work material, grinding speed, area of contact and capability of the machine. [GP p.9]
- [GRD-F060] Structure is the spacing (porosity) between abrasive grains; a lower number means a denser wheel, a higher number a more open wheel. [GP p.10; GPPT s.14; CG p.3]
- [GRD-F061] Open-structure wheels are used for rough grinding and soft, ductile materials; dense-structure wheels for hard and brittle materials and finer finish. [GP p.10] (corrects notebook)
- [GRD-F062] The bond is the adhesive material that holds the abrasive grains together in the form of a wheel. [GP p.11; NB p.8]
- [GRD-F063] Bond symbols: V = vitrified, S = silicate, E = shellac, B = resinoid, R = rubber, M = metal, O = oxychloride. [GP p.11; CG p.3; NB p.8]
- [GRD-F064] Vitrified (V) bond is the most common bond: strong, rigid, porous and unaffected by water, oils and acids. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F065] Resinoid (B) bonds are used for high-speed wheels and cut-off wheels; rubber (R) bonds for thin cut-off wheels and regulating wheels of centreless grinders. [theory: Hajra Choudhury; GP p.5]
- [GRD-F066] Example A 85 I 5 V (notebook): aluminium oxide, fine grain (85), medium grade (I), structure 5, vitrified bond. [NB p.8]
- [GRD-F067] Example C 32 _ 8 B (notebook): silicon carbide, medium grain (32), structure 8, resinoid bond. [NB p.8] (grade letter illegible — not used)

## 5. Wheel condition: dressing, truing, glazing, loading, balancing
- [GRD-F068] Dressing removes the dull outer layer of grains and embedded chips from the wheel face to expose fresh sharp grains, using a dresser. [NB p.8; GPPT s.17]
- [GRD-F069] Truing restores the wheel's correct geometric shape (makes it run concentric with its axis), usually with a diamond tool. [GPPT s.17; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F070] Glazing: the abrasive grains become blunt and the wheel surface turns smooth and shiny, so the wheel stops cutting. [NB p.8–9; GPPT s.16]
- [GRD-F071] A blunt (glazed) wheel is identified by its shiny, smooth abrasive surface. [NB p.9]
- [GRD-F072] Glazing is typically caused by using too hard a wheel (grains not released); the remedy is dressing or a softer wheel. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F073] Loading: chips of the work metal fill (clog) the pores of the wheel, mostly when grinding soft ductile metals; remedy is dressing and a more open wheel. [GPPT s.16; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F074] Self-sharpening: blunt grains break away from the bond on their own, exposing new sharp grains. [NB p.9]
- [GRD-F075] The grinding wheel must be balanced (e.g. on a balancing stand) before use to avoid vibration and chatter. [GPPT s.15–16]
- [GRD-F076] Grinding defects: burn marks (overheating from excess heat or dull wheel), chatter marks (imbalance or loose set-up), glazing, poor finish (wrong grit, speed or feed), and cracks in wheel or work (excess pressure or wrong wheel). [GPPT s.16]

## 6. Advantages, limitations and applications
- [GRD-F077] Advantages of grinding: good surface finish, good dimensional accuracy, and it can finish hard (even hardened) materials — hardness does not matter. [NB p.9; GL p.8]
- [GRD-F078] Disadvantages of grinding: costly, high specific energy (high power), and coolant is essential. [NB p.9]
- [GRD-F079] Grinding can achieve micron-level accuracy and mirror-like finish, sharpen tools, finish hard materials and remove small burrs. [GPPT s.7]
- [GRD-F080] Applications: automobile parts (shafts, gears, bearings), tool sharpening, aerospace (turbine blades), scale removal in steel industry, electronics (flat semiconductor surfaces). [GPPT s.8]

## 7. The ME-106 grinding job
- [GRD-F081] Objective (1): to give surface finish to a plane (flat) surface using a surface grinding machine. [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F082] Objective (2): to give surface finish to a cylindrical surface using a cylindrical grinding machine. [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F083] Workpieces: a mild-steel cylindrical rod (cylindrical grinding) and a cubical/rectangular block (surface grinding). [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F084] Tools listed: chuck, mild-steel cylindrical rod, cubical block, Vernier caliper, grinding wheel, coolant (water-based) and workpiece holder. [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F085] In the lab cylindrical grinding set-up, the job is held between centres (dead centre at the tailstock). [LS p.7]

## 8. Procedure (our lab)
### Surface grinding
- [GRD-F086] Step 1: take the raw material whose surface is to be finished. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F087] Step 2: clean the table (bed/magnetic chuck) before placing the workpiece. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F088] Step 3: place magnetic parallels around the workpiece so it does not shift during grinding. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F089] Step 4: switch on the magnetic chuck to hold the workpiece on the table. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F090] Step 5: set the wheel about an inch above the workpiece and ensure it is not touching the work before starting. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F091] Step 6: adjust the handwheels as required and start the machine with coolant. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F092] Step 7: after the job, wait until the wheel stops, then remove the workpiece by demagnetising the chuck. [LS p.8]
### Cylindrical grinding
- [GRD-F093] Step 1: take the rough cylindrical workpiece and hold it well-centred on the machine. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F094] Step 2: adjust the wheel position before starting the machine, then start the coolant. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F095] Step 3: take a trial cut and check with an outside micrometer to correct the size. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F096] Step 4: dress the wheel for a fine finish and finish the job. [LS p.8]
- [GRD-F097] Step 5: after the work is finished, remove the workpiece and switch off the machine. [LS p.8]

## 9. Precautions (grinding shop)
- [GRD-F098] Always wear an apron and leather (safety) shoes. [LS p.7; GPPT s.2]
- [GRD-F099] Do not wear loose accessories such as bracelets, chains, wristwatches or loose threads. [LS p.7; GPPT s.2]
- [GRD-F100] Tie long hair back. [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F101] Make sure the workpiece is firmly fixed (clamped / magnetised) on the machine. [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F102] Keep a proper distance from the wheel and machine; stand to one side of the wheel when starting. [LS p.7; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F103] Check that the wheel clears the work before starting the grinder. [LS p.7]
- [GRD-F104] Stay attentive and do not operate the machine without the instructor's supervision. [GPPT s.2]
- [GRD-F105] Wear safety goggles and keep the wheel guard in place, as sparks and abrasive particles fly off. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F106] Never exceed the maximum safe speed marked on the wheel, and check the wheel for cracks (ring test) before mounting. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 10. Grinding tools and accessories (standard practice)
- [GRD-F107] A single-point diamond dresser is used for precise dressing and truing; a star-wheel (Huntington) dresser is used for rough dressing of coarse wheels. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F108] Wheels are balanced on a balancing stand (arbor on knife edges or rollers), shifting balance weights in the flange until the wheel stays in any position. [theory: Hajra Choudhury; GPPT s.15]
- [GRD-F109] A magnetic chuck holds only ferromagnetic work (steel, cast iron); non-magnetic work such as aluminium or brass must be held in a vice or fixture. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F110] Grinding coolant is usually a water-based soluble-oil emulsion that cools the work, washes away swarf and reduces wheel loading. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [GRD-F111] When mounting a wheel, flanges of equal size and soft paper/rubber washers (blotters) are used on both sides, and the nut is tightened only enough to hold the wheel. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F112] The ring test: a vitrified wheel is suspended and tapped lightly with a non-metallic (wooden) mallet; a clear ring means sound, a dull thud indicates a crack. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [GRD-F113] The work-rest (tool rest) on a bench/pedestal grinder must be kept close to the wheel (about 2–3 mm) so the work cannot be dragged in. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

# Electroplating Practice (Activity 09 — Fitting & Electroplating shop, near ECE Dept.)

Source keys: `EP` = Electroplating.pdf (= Electroplating for workshop practice.pdf; lecture slides, page numbers); `BOARD` = Electroplating Lab.jpg (lab blackboard); `ELS` = Electroplating Log Sheet.jpg (2025 log sheet). `theory:` = Hajra Choudhury Vol. I; Dini, *Electrodeposition*; standard physics/chemistry (Faraday's laws).

## 1. Principle
- [ELP-F001] Electroplating is the application of a thin layer of metal coating on a metallic (or other conducting) substrate, to improve appearance and/or surface properties. [EP p.2]
- [ELP-F002] Electroplating is based on electrolysis. [EP p.2]
- [ELP-F003] The workpiece (substrate to be coated) is made the cathode (connected to the negative terminal of the DC supply). [EP p.2–3]
- [ELP-F004] The anode is usually the metal to be deposited (connected to the positive terminal). [EP p.2–3]
- [ELP-F005] Metal ions (positive) in the electrolyte move towards the cathode, gain electrons and deposit as metal on the workpiece; the anode dissolves to replenish the ions. [theory: Dini]
- [ELP-F006] Electroplating needs a direct-current (DC) supply, not AC. [EP p.3]
- [ELP-F007] An electrolysis (plating) cell consists of: a DC power source of variable voltage and current, high-capacity current-carrying conductors, current and voltage meters, the cathode (work), the anode (plating metal) and the electrolyte (bath) in a tank. [EP p.3]
- [ELP-F008] The ammeter is connected in series and the voltmeter across the cell. [EP p.3; theory: physics]
- [ELP-F009] The electrolyte (bath) is a solution containing ions of the metal to be deposited. [EP p.3; theory: Dini]

## 2. Faraday's laws and numericals
- [ELP-F010] Faraday's first law: the mass of metal deposited is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed (current × time). [EP p.2]
- [ELP-F011] Current and time are the most important parameters controlling the amount deposited. [EP p.2]
- [ELP-F012] 96,500 coulombs (1 faraday) deposit one gram-equivalent of a metal. [EP p.2]
- [ELP-F013] Mass deposited m = (M × I × t) / (n × F), where M = atomic mass, n = valency, I = current (A), t = time (s), F = 96,500 C/mol. [theory: Faraday's laws]
- [ELP-F014] Faraday's second law: for the same charge, the masses deposited are proportional to the chemical equivalents (M/n) of the metals. [theory: Faraday's laws]
- [ELP-F015] For copper (Cu²⁺), mass deposited ≈ 1.185 × I × t grams, with I in amperes and t in hours. [EP p.2]
- [ELP-F016] For nickel (Ni²⁺), mass deposited ≈ 1.095 × I × t grams (I in A, t in h). [theory: Faraday's laws]
- [ELP-F017] Electrochemical equivalent Z = M/(n F) g/C; m = Z I t. [theory: Faraday's laws]
- [ELP-F018] Current density (A per unit area of cathode) controls deposit quality: too high a current density gives burnt, rough, powdery deposits. [BOARD; theory: Dini]

## 3. Cleaning (pre-treatment)
- [ELP-F019] The job must be cleaned before plating for better adhesion of the coating, a smooth surface and good appearance. [EP p.4]
- [ELP-F020] Mechanical cleaning removes rust and burrs, using a metal (wire) abrasive brush or emery paper (scratch brushing). [EP p.4; BOARD]
- [ELP-F021] Buffing/polishing is done on a buffing wheel (cotton mop) with emery (abrasive) compound. [EP p.4; BOARD; ELS]
- [ELP-F022] Buffing is polishing/smoothening/shining a metal piece by rubbing it against a rotating fabric (cloth) wheel periodically charged with fine abrasive compound. [EP p.5]
- [ELP-F023] Buffing compounds (generally solid bars) are fine abrasive particles (alumina, SiC, iron oxide, chromium oxide) in a binder such as fatty acid, greaseless wax or soap. [EP p.5, p.12]
- [ELP-F024] Buffing wheel types: spiral sewn, loose buffs, concentric sewn and flannel. [EP p.12]
- [ELP-F025] In buffing the pressure is applied by hand; more pressure is faster but wastes compound, low pressure gives a finer finish. [EP p.12]
- [ELP-F026] Chemical cleaning in the lab is electro-chemical cleaning plus chemical cleaning. [EP p.6]
- [ELP-F027] Electrochemical cleaning is the reverse of electroplating — the workpiece is made the anode. [EP p.6]
- [ELP-F028] In electrochemical cleaning the oxidation reaction 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻ occurs at the work surface; the oxygen gas released scrubs off rust and contaminants. [EP p.6]
- [ELP-F029] Chemical-cleaning compounds: sodium carbonate; sodium silicate (protects aluminium against soda); trisodium phosphate (removes hydrocarbons like grease). [EP p.6]
- [ELP-F030] The lab chemical-cleaning bath contains sodium carbonate, sodium hydroxide, trisodium phosphate, sodium cyanide, sodium silicate and water. [BOARD]
- [ELP-F031] Lab chemical-cleaning conditions: about 6 V, 10–15 A, for about 5 minutes. [BOARD]
- [ELP-F032] After chemical cleaning the job is washed with water and pumice powder. [BOARD; ELS]
- [ELP-F033] Degreasing removes oil and grease; pickling (dilute acid dip) removes oxide scale and rust; rinsing between stages prevents carry-over of chemicals. [theory: Dini]

## 4. Copper plating
- [ELP-F034] Copper plating gives a corrosion-resistant coating and provides a base (undercoat) for other plating. [EP p.7]
- [ELP-F035] Copper can be electroplated from an acid bath or a cyanide bath; the cyanide bath is popular. [EP p.7]
- [ELP-F036] Cyanide copper plating works on steels, zinc, aluminium, magnesium, nickel and lead; it gives good adhesion and a decorative finish. [EP p.7]
- [ELP-F037] A cyanide copper strike protects the base metal (steel) from acid attack during subsequent acid plating such as nickel plating. [EP p.7]
- [ELP-F038] Copper cyanide (CuCN) is the source of copper; it is not soluble in water, so sodium or potassium cyanide is added to form soluble complexes. [EP p.8]
- [ELP-F039] Excess NaCN/KCN gives good-quality deposits and good anode corrosion (dissolution). [EP p.8]
- [ELP-F040] Caustic soda (NaOH) or caustic potash (KOH) is added to the copper bath to increase conductivity, reduce attack on steel anode containers and reduce cyanide decomposition. [EP p.8]
- [ELP-F041] Rochelle salt gives a fine-grained copper deposit; Na₂CO₃/K₂CO₃ improve conductivity; excess carbonate (from cyanide decomposition) can be removed with lime. [EP p.8]
- [ELP-F042] Lab copper-plating conditions: copper cyanide + sodium cyanide bath, about 2–6 V, for about 25–30 seconds. [BOARD; ELS]
- [ELP-F043] Lab copper-plating current is a few amperes (about 5–8 A on the log sheet, 6–8 A on the board). [BOARD; ELS]
- [ELP-F044] The job is kept in the copper bath until it turns reddish (copper-coloured), then washed again. [ELS]

## 5. Nickel plating
- [ELP-F045] Nickel plating improves wear, heat and corrosion resistance and gives a bright decorative appearance. [EP p.10]
- [ELP-F046] Nickel bath (Watts type): nickel sulphate (main source of Ni ions), nickel chloride (increases conductivity and helps anode dissolution), boric acid (controls pH), plus brighteners and anti-pitting agents. [EP p.10]
- [ELP-F047] Sulphuric acid is added regularly to the nickel bath because hydrogen ions are lost as H₂ gas. [EP p.10]
- [ELP-F048] The anode in nickel plating is nickel metal — lumps of various shapes, or small pieces kept in a titanium basket. [EP p.10]
- [ELP-F049] Lab nickel-plating conditions: about 1.5–3 V and 5–7 minutes; the log sheet gives a current of about 5 A (the board writes the current density as 5 A/sq ft). [BOARD; ELS]
- [ELP-F050] In the lab job only half of the copper-plated workpiece is dipped in the nickel bath, so the job shows both copper and nickel layers. [ELS]
- [ELP-F051] Ingredients written for the lab nickel bath include nickel salts, boric acid and water. [BOARD]

## 6. The ME-106 electroplating job
- [ELP-F052] Name of operation: electroplating of a mild-steel workpiece. [ELS]
- [ELP-F053] Tools/materials listed: mild-steel workpiece, emery paper, polishing machine, pumice powder, electroplating solution, wire. [ELS]
- [ELP-F054] The job is tied with (copper) wire to hang it in the solution and make electrical contact. [ELS]
- [ELP-F055] The job drawing shows a workpiece with one part copper-plated and one part nickel-plated, the solution box (tank with DC panel) and the polishing machine. [ELS]

## 7. Procedure (our lab)
- [ELP-F056] Step 1 — Mechanical cleaning: clean the workpiece with emery paper and polish it with cotton mop and emery compound on the polishing machine. [ELS; BOARD]
- [ELP-F057] Step 2 — Chemical cleaning: tie the job with wire and dip it in the cleaning solution, then wash with water and pumice powder. [ELS; BOARD]
- [ELP-F058] Step 3 — Copper plating: dip the job in the copper solution (2–6 V, 25–30 s) until it becomes reddish, then wash. [ELS]
- [ELP-F059] Step 4 — Nickel plating: dip half of the job in the nickel solution (1.5–3 V, ~5 A, 5–7 min) and take it out — electroplating is done. [ELS]
- [ELP-F060] Correct lab sequence: mechanical cleaning → polishing (buffing) → chemical cleaning → washing → copper plating → washing → nickel plating. [ELS; BOARD]
- [ELP-F061] The general industrial sequence is: degreasing/cleaning → rinsing → pickling → rinsing → plating → rinsing → drying. [theory: Dini]

## 8. Precautions
- [ELP-F062] Wear proper shoes and an apron in the workshop. [ELS]
- [ELP-F063] Handle the workpiece with care while cleaning and polishing. [ELS]
- [ELP-F064] Operate the electroplating equipment with caution, and keep an appropriate distance from the machines. [ELS]
- [ELP-F065] Cyanide is highly poisonous; airborne cyanide must be limited to 5 mg/m³ averaged over an 8-hour shift. [EP p.9]
- [ELP-F066] Cyanide dust can form when handling cyanide salts, making additions or new solutions. [EP p.9]
- [ELP-F067] The plating solution should not be touched; plated parts must be rinsed thoroughly; hands must be washed properly. [EP p.9]
- [ELP-F068] Copper dust in large quantity (from grinding/polishing) is also dangerous. [EP p.9]
- [ELP-F069] Nickel and nickel salts can cause dermatitis on skin contact and may affect the lungs on long-term inhalation. [EP p.11]
- [ELP-F070] Protective clothing, eye protection and gloves are mandatory around plating baths; wash at once after skin contact. [EP p.11]
- [ELP-F071] Plating areas need local exhaust ventilation; take special care during bath make-up and maintenance. [EP p.11]
- [ELP-F072] Do not eat, drink or smoke near the plating line. [EP p.11]
- [ELP-F073] Never pour water into concentrated acid (add acid to water slowly). [theory: lab safety]

## 9. Defects, advantages, applications
- [ELP-F074] Poor cleaning causes poor adhesion — the plated layer peels or blisters. [EP p.4; theory: Dini]
- [ELP-F075] Too high a current density gives burnt (dark, rough) deposits; hydrogen bubbles sticking to the cathode cause pitting. [theory: Dini]
- [ELP-F076] Throwing power is the ability of a bath to deposit a uniform coating on irregular (recessed) surfaces. [theory: Dini]
- [ELP-F077] Purposes of electroplating: corrosion protection, decorative appearance, wear resistance, building up worn parts, and improving conductivity or solderability. [EP p.2, p.7, p.10; theory: Dini]
- [ELP-F078] Applications: decorative and protective coatings on automobile parts, bathroom fittings, utensils, jewellery, electrical contacts and machine parts. [theory: Dini]
- [ELP-F079] Common plating metals: copper, nickel, chromium, zinc (galvanising), tin, silver and gold. [theory: Dini]

# Shaping Practice (Activity 10 — Machine Shop)

Source keys: `LS` = Log Sheets ME-106.pdf p.3–4 (shaping log sheet); `NB` = Manufacturing_Notes_2023.pdf; `SH` = shaping.pdf (lecture slides); `CHART` = Shaping Workshop.jpg (wall chart "Parts of a standard shaper"). `theory:` = Hajra Choudhury Vol. II; P.N. Rao.

## 1. Principle
- [SHP-F001] A shaper is a reciprocating-type machine tool that uses a single-point cutting tool. [SH p.5]
- [SHP-F002] The shaper converts the rotary motion of the driver (motor/bull gear) into the reciprocating motion of the ram. [SH p.11; NB p.19]
- [SHP-F003] A shaper mainly produces flat surfaces, which may be horizontal, vertical or inclined; it can also produce slots, grooves and (on modern machines) curved surfaces. [SH p.5]
- [SHP-F004] Metal is removed only in the forward (cutting) stroke; the return stroke is an idle stroke with no cutting. [SH p.11]
- [SHP-F005] A quick-return mechanism makes the idle return stroke faster than the cutting stroke, reducing total machining time. [SH p.11]
- [SHP-F006] Most shapers use the crank and slotted-lever quick-return mechanism. [SH p.12]
- [SHP-F007] In shaping the tool reciprocates and the work is fed (crosswise) between strokes; in planing the work reciprocates under a stationary tool. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F008] Machining is a metal-removal process in which metal is removed as chips by a cutting tool to obtain the desired size and shape. [SH p.2]
- [SHP-F009] During cutting the tool shears off the excess metal; the chip undergoes plastic deformation, and chip thickness is always greater than the uncut chip thickness. [SH p.3; NB p.18]
- [SHP-F010] Two relative motions are needed in a machine tool: primary motion (cutting velocity) and secondary motion (feed velocity, gradual feeding to the uncut portion). [SH p.8]
- [SHP-F011] The generatrix is the line generated by the primary (cutting) motion; the directrix is the line generated by the secondary (feed) motion. [SH p.8–9]
- [SHP-F012] In orthogonal cutting, metal shears along the shear plane ahead of the tool; the rake angle is measured on the tool face (rake plane). [NB p.18]

## 2. Quick-return mechanism
- [SHP-F013] Parts of the crank and slotted-lever mechanism: bull gear (crank), crank pin, sliding block, slotted lever (link), pivot (fulcrum at bottom), link/connecting rod to the ram, driving pinion. [CHART; NB p.18–19; SH p.12]
- [SHP-F014] The bull gear is driven by a small driving pinion (gear) from the motor. [CHART]
- [SHP-F015] The crank pin carries a sliding block that slides in the slotted lever, making the lever oscillate about its pivot. [CHART; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F016] In the standard shaper chart, the crank turns through 240° during the cutting stroke and 120° during the return stroke. [CHART; NB p.19]
- [SHP-F017] Since the crank rotates at constant speed, time ratio = cutting angle / return angle; for 240°/120° the ratio is 2 : 1 (return twice as fast). [NB p.19; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F018] The quick-return ratio of shapers usually lies between about 3:2 and 2:1. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F019] The stroke length is changed by changing the radius of the crank pin (moving the sliding block) on the bull gear. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F020] The position of stroke is changed by moving the ram relative to its connection (ram adjustment / locking handle). [SH p.6; theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 3. Machine parts
- [SHP-F021] Parts of the shaper (lecture figure): clapper box, tool slide, ram adjustment nut, ram locking handle, ram, tool, tool post, vice, table, cross feed, cross rail, vertical feed, column, driving pulley, base. [SH p.6]
- [SHP-F022] Parts on the lab wall chart: head (can swing on front of the ram), clapper box, tool, ram, table, front support for table. [CHART; NB p.19]
- [SHP-F023] The ram is the reciprocating member that carries the tool head at its front end. [CHART; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F024] The tool head (head) is mounted at the front of the ram and can be swivelled for angular (inclined) cuts. [CHART; NB p.19]
- [SHP-F025] The tool head has a down-feed screw (vertical feed handle) to give depth of cut. [SH p.6; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F026] The clapper box holds the tool post; the clapper block is hinged so the tool lifts (swings up) during the return stroke and does not drag on the work. [CHART; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F027] The table carries the vice (or work) and is mounted on the cross rail; it is moved crosswise for feed and vertically for setting. [SH p.6; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F028] The cross rail is mounted on the front of the column and carries the table; the cross-feed screw moves the table horizontally. [SH p.6; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F029] The column (body) is a hollow box casting on the base that houses the drive mechanism and carries the ram guides. [SH p.6; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F030] The base supports the whole machine; the front support (table support) steadies the table. [SH p.6; CHART]
- [SHP-F031] The work is held in a machine vice (plain vice) on the table. [NB p.21; LS p.3]

## 4. Classification of shapers
- [SHP-F032] By ram-driving mechanism: crank type, geared type, hydraulic type. [SH p.10]
- [SHP-F033] By design of table: standard shaper, universal shaper. [SH p.10]
- [SHP-F034] By position and travel of ram: horizontal, vertical, travelling-head type. [SH p.10]
- [SHP-F035] By type of cutting stroke: push type, draw type. [SH p.10]
- [SHP-F036] In a push-type shaper metal is cut as the ram moves away from the column (most common); in a draw type it cuts on the stroke towards the column. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 5. Cutting tool and tool materials
- [SHP-F037] The shaping tool in our lab is a single-point cutting tool made of high-speed steel (HSS). [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F038] Parts of a single-point tool: shank, face, base, heel, nose, side (major) cutting edge, end (minor) cutting edge, major flank, minor flank. [SH p.7]
- [SHP-F039] Properties of a cutting-tool material: hot hardness, toughness, wear resistance, chemical stability, shock resistance, low friction, favourable cost. [SH p.4]
- [SHP-F040] Cutting-tool materials: carbon tool steel, HSS, cemented carbide, ceramic, cubic boron nitride (CBN), diamond (roughly in order of increasing hot hardness). [SH p.4; theory: Kalpakjian]
- [SHP-F041] Rake angle reduces cutting force and helps chip flow; clearance (relief) angle prevents the flank rubbing the work. [NB p.18; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F042] Shaper tools are robust with smaller rake/clearance than lathe tools because they take intermittent cuts with impact at the start of every stroke. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 6. The ME-106 shaping job
- [SHP-F043] Objective: to reduce the given block to the desired dimensions 38 mm × 21 mm × 73 mm. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F044] Finished block: length 73 mm, width 38 mm, height (thickness) 21 mm. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F045] Tools listed: single-point cutting tool (HSS), scale with base (stand), marking block, try square, hammer, chalk and dot punch. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F046] The least count of the down-feed (scale wheel above the tool head) in our lab is 0.05 mm. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F047] Marking tools used: chalk, scale with stand, marking block and try square; the dot punch and hammer make the marks permanent. [LS p.3; NB p.21]

## 7. Procedure (our lab)
- [SHP-F048] Notebook procedure: chalk & marking → punching (to make the marking clear) → lock the job in the plain machine vice → use the cross-feed handle to align the job with the tool → use the depth handle to give depth of cut → start the machine. [NB p.21]
- [SHP-F049] Log-sheet step 1: mark the dimensions on the block using the scale with stand, marking block, try square, hammer and dot punch. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F050] Log-sheet step 2: fix the block tightly in the vice (clamp). [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F051] Step 3: using the ratchet-and-crank (feed) mechanism bring the tool near the block until it just touches. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F052] Step 4: with the wheel (handle) above the tool head, lower the tool by 1 mm on the scale for the first cut. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F053] Step 5: start the machine (push button). [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F054] Step 6: after the first removal, with the machine OFF, lower the head again by 0.5–1 mm as required; repeat until one side reaches the size. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F055] Step 7: switch off, remove waste (chips), and repeat the procedure for the other sides. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F056] The crank on the ratchet (automatic feed) must be adjusted so the table moves in the correct direction while the machine runs. [LS p.3]

## 8. Precautions (shaping shop)
- [SHP-F057] Wear an apron. [LS p.4]
- [SHP-F058] Never put hands on the machine (near the ram/tool) while it is running. [LS p.4]
- [SHP-F059] Do not lower the tool head more than about 2 mm (depth of cut) after touching the block. [LS p.4]
- [SHP-F060] Make sure the ratchet (feed) system is set so the table moves in the correct direction. [LS p.4]
- [SHP-F061] Give the depth of cut only when the machine is stopped. [LS p.3]
- [SHP-F062] Set the stroke length and position so the tool clears the work at both ends of the stroke. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F063] Do not stand in front of (in line with) the ram during operation. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F064] Clamp the job and tool firmly; remove chips with a brush, not by hand. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 9. Advantages, limitations, applications
- [SHP-F065] Advantages: simple, cheap single-point tools; easy set-up; good for flat surfaces, keyways, slots and small jobs. [theory: P.N. Rao]
- [SHP-F066] Limitations: slow (cutting only in one stroke), low production rate, mainly suited to small/medium work. [SH p.11; theory: P.N. Rao]
- [SHP-F067] Applications: machining flat horizontal, vertical and inclined surfaces, V-blocks, keyways, slots and dovetails. [SH p.5; theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 10. Shaper numericals (standard)
- [SHP-F068] Shaper cutting speed V = L·N·(1 + m) / 1000 m/min, where L = stroke length (mm), N = number of double strokes per minute, and m = return time ÷ cutting time. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F069] Number of strokes needed = width of the surface ÷ feed per stroke; machining time = number of strokes ÷ strokes per minute. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [SHP-F070] With constant crank speed, the cutting and return angles are 360° − 2θ and 2θ, where cos θ = r/d (r = crank-pin radius, d = distance between crank centre and slotted-lever pivot). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

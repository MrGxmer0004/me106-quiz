# Un-conventional Machining Practice (Activity 12 — Automobile Shop): Wire-cut EDM + Fiber Laser Cutting

Source keys: `UCM` = Unconventional.pdf (log sheet, 2 pages). `theory:` = P.K. Mishra, *Nonconventional Machining*; Kalpakjian, *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology*; Makino EDM wire tutorial (makino.com); *Complete EDM Handbook* (reliableedm.com); Laser Focus World, "Cutting thick section steel with fiber lasers"; NPTEL/IARE non-traditional machining notes.

## 1. Unconventional machining — general
- [UCM-F001] Unconventional (non-traditional) machining removes material using energy forms other than a sharp cutting tool in direct contact — e.g. electrical sparks, laser light, chemicals, electrochemical action, abrasive/water jets. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F002] They are needed for very hard or brittle materials, complex shapes, very small or deep holes, and thin delicate parts where cutting forces must be avoided. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F003] Classification by energy: mechanical (USM, AJM, WJM), thermal/electrothermal (EDM, WEDM, LBM, EBM, PAM), electrochemical (ECM) and chemical (chemical machining/milling). [theory: IARE UCMP notes; P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F004] In thermal processes (EDM, LBM, EBM, PAM) material is removed by local melting and vaporisation. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F005] ECM removes metal by anodic dissolution — it is the reverse of electroplating. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F006] In most unconventional processes the tool need not be harder than the work. [theory: P.K. Mishra]

## 2. Wire-cut EDM — principle
- [UCM-F007] In wire EDM, material is removed by thermal erosion: a rapid series of pulsed electrical sparks between a thin wire electrode and the workpiece melts and vaporises tiny particles of metal. [UCM p.1; theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F008] There is no mechanical contact between the wire and the workpiece, so there is practically no cutting force. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F009] If the wire touches the workpiece it causes a short circuit, so a small spark gap is maintained. [UCM p.1; theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F010] The spark gap is kept constant by a servo control system (typically about 0.025–0.075 mm). [theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F011] If the gap is too large no spark forms; if too small the wire shorts against the work. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F012] A pulsed DC voltage is applied between wire and workpiece; during pulse-on time (Ton) the discharge occurs and material is removed. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F013] During pulse-off time (Toff) the dielectric de-ionises (recovers its insulation) and the debris is flushed away. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F014] The workpiece must be electrically conductive; its hardness does not matter, so hardened steel, carbide and titanium can be cut. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F015] The wire moves along the programmed profile and completes the cut; the path is controlled by CNC (part programming). [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F016] For an internal profile a start hole must first be drilled so the wire can be threaded through the job. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F017] WEDM cuts 2-D (through) profiles; the upper and lower guides can be tilted for tapered cuts. [theory: Kalpakjian]

## 3. Wire-cut EDM — elements and parameters
- [UCM-F018] The dielectric in wire EDM is usually deionised water. [theory: Makino EDM wire tutorial]
- [UCM-F019] Die-sinking EDM usually uses a hydrocarbon oil (e.g. kerosene) as dielectric. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F020] Functions of the dielectric: insulates until breakdown, concentrates the spark, cools the zone, and flushes away the debris. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F021] The dielectric is continuously filtered and deionised (tap water is too conductive). [theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F022] Wire electrode material is usually brass (also coated/zinc-coated brass, copper, molybdenum or tungsten). [theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F023] The wire is very thin, about 0.05–0.30 mm in diameter (commonly 0.25 mm). [theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F024] The wire is fed continuously from a supply spool to a take-up and is used only once (not reused), because it erodes. [theory: Makino EDM wire tutorial]
- [UCM-F025] The wire runs between upper and lower wire guides above and below the workpiece. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F026] Kerf width in wire EDM = wire diameter + 2 × spark gap. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F027] The wire offset (path offset) = wire radius + spark gap. [theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F028] Main WEDM parameters: pulse-on time (Ton), pulse-off time (Toff), peak current, voltage, wire tension, wire feed rate and dielectric flushing pressure. [UCM p.1–2; theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F029] Higher spark energy (higher current, longer Ton) increases cutting rate but gives a rougher surface and larger recast layer. [theory: P.K. Mishra]
- [UCM-F030] Wire tension must be correct: too low causes wire vibration/inaccuracy; too high causes wire breakage. [UCM p.2; theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F031] Inadequate flushing is a main cause of wire breakage and poor finish. [theory: Complete EDM Handbook]
- [UCM-F032] Each spark leaves a small crater on the work surface; the re-solidified layer is the recast layer, below which is a heat-affected zone. [theory: Kalpakjian]

## 4. WEDM — advantages, limitations, applications, comparison
- [UCM-F033] WEDM advantages: complex 2-D profiles, high accuracy and finish, no burrs, no cutting force (thin/delicate parts), cuts hardened materials. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F034] WEDM limitations: only electrically conductive materials, slow material removal, recast layer/HAZ, wire cost. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F035] WEDM applications: dies and punches, tool and die making, extrusion dies, gears, aerospace and medical parts. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F036] Die-sinking EDM uses a shaped (e.g. copper/graphite) electrode to sink a 3-D cavity; wire EDM uses a moving thin wire to cut through-profiles. [theory: P.K. Mishra]

## 5. Fiber laser cutting — principle
- [UCM-F037] In laser cutting a focused high-intensity laser beam heats the metal locally until it melts or vaporises; a coaxial assist-gas jet blows the molten metal out of the kerf. [theory: Laser Focus World; Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F038] LASER stands for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F039] A fiber laser is a solid-state laser whose gain medium is an optical fiber doped with ytterbium. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F040] Fiber lasers emit at about 1.06 µm (1064 nm); CO₂ lasers emit at about 10.6 µm. [theory: industry sources (GWK, Xometry)]
- [UCM-F041] Metals absorb the shorter 1.06 µm fiber-laser wavelength much better than the 10.6 µm CO₂ wavelength, so fiber lasers are preferred for metal sheet. [theory: industry sources]
- [UCM-F042] CO₂ lasers are widely used for non-metals such as wood, acrylic and plastics. [theory: industry sources]
- [UCM-F043] The beam is delivered through a flexible optical fiber to the cutting head; the cutting path is CNC-programmed. [UCM p.2; theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F044] Laser cutting is non-contact, so there is no tool wear and no cutting force. [theory: Kalpakjian]

## 6. Fiber laser — assist gas and parameters
- [UCM-F045] Oxygen assist gas is used for mild steel: its exothermic reaction with iron adds heat, allowing faster/thicker cutting, but leaves an oxide layer on the cut edge. [theory: Laser Focus World]
- [UCM-F046] Nitrogen (inert) assist gas is used for stainless steel and aluminium; the laser supplies all the energy and the edges are clean and oxide-free. [theory: Laser Focus World]
- [UCM-F047] Compressed air is a cheap assist-gas option for thin sheets. [theory: Laser Focus World]
- [UCM-F048] Assist gas also protects the focusing lens from spatter and cools the cut. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F049] Main laser-cutting parameters: laser power, cutting speed, focal position, assist-gas type and pressure, nozzle diameter and stand-off distance. [UCM p.2; theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F050] The cutting head contains a collimator and a focusing lens that focuses the beam to a small spot; the nozzle directs assist gas coaxially. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F051] Stand-off distance is the gap between the nozzle tip and the workpiece surface. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F052] Laser kerf is narrow (a few tenths of a mm) and the heat-affected zone (HAZ) is small. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F053] Laser-cut quality terms: kerf width, HAZ, dross (re-solidified metal stuck to the bottom edge), striations (lines on the cut face). [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F054] Cutting too fast leaves the cut incomplete and causes dross; cutting too slowly widens the kerf and HAZ. [theory: Kalpakjian]

## 7. Fiber laser — advantages, limitations, safety
- [UCM-F055] Advantages: high speed and precision, narrow kerf, small HAZ, no tool wear, intricate shapes, easy automation. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F056] Limitations: high equipment cost, thickness limit, highly reflective metals (copper, brass) can be difficult, fumes produced. [theory: Kalpakjian]
- [UCM-F057] Laser safety: wear laser safety goggles of the correct wavelength rating, never look into the beam, keep the enclosure closed, use fume extraction, keep hands off the bed while cutting, watch for fire. [theory: laser safety practice]

## 8. The ME-106 lab jobs
- [UCM-F058] Objectives: to study wire electrical discharge machining and to study the fiber laser cutting machine. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F059] The WEDM job is a square of 10 mm × 10 mm with 5 mm thickness. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F060] The fiber-laser job is a ring (annular shape) cut from metal sheet. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F061] Tools listed for WEDM: wire-cut EDM machine, clamping tool, clamping wire, power supply, dielectric fluid supply, part programming, wire electrode. [UCM p.1]

## 9. Procedure — wire EDM (our lab)
- [UCM-F062] Step 1: clamp the workpiece properly on the EDM machine. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F063] Step 2: fix and tension the wire electrode. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F064] Step 3: set the required machining parameters. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F065] Step 4: supply the dielectric fluid to the machining zone. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F066] Step 5: enter the required profile through part programming. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F067] Step 6: maintain a small spark gap between wire and workpiece. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F068] Step 7: apply pulsed electrical voltage between the wire and the workpiece. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F069] Steps 8–9: material is removed by the heat generated by the sparks; during Ton the discharge occurs and material is removed. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F070] Step 10: the wire moves along the programmed profile and completes the cut. [UCM p.1]
- [UCM-F071] Step 11: remove and inspect the machined job. [UCM p.1]

## 10. Procedure — fiber laser (our lab)
- [UCM-F072] Step 1: clamp the metal sheet properly on the machine bed. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F073] Step 2: set the required cutting profile in the CNC controller. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F074] Step 3: set laser power, cutting speed and assist gas. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F075] Step 4: focus the laser beam on the workpiece (using the remote). [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F076] Step 5: check that the laser will cut at the correct place by a dry run (dry cutting). [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F077] Step 6: start the laser beam and cut along the programmed path. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F078] Step 7: remove the cut piece and inspect the profile. [UCM p.2]

## 11. Precautions (our lab)
- [UCM-F079] Clamp the workpiece properly. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F080] Maintain correct wire tension and spark gap. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F081] Ensure a continuous supply of dielectric fluid. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F082] Set Ton, Toff and peak current correctly. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F083] Set the correct laser power and cutting speed. [UCM p.2]
- [UCM-F084] Check the cutting accuracy by a dry (laser) run before actual cutting. [UCM p.2]

## 1. Definition and purpose
- [FIT-F001] Fitting is the process of shaping, finishing and assembling metal components manually with hand tools to achieve accurate dimensions and smooth surfaces. [PPT s.4]
- [FIT-F002] Fitting work is bench work: it achieves accuracy through filing, sawing, drilling and tapping, and is normally done with the job held in a bench vice. [PPT s.4]
- [FIT-F003] Fitting teaches manual precision before automation and is needed in prototyping, maintenance, repair and R&D. [PPT s.4, s.11]
- [FIT-F004] Fitting builds skills of dimensional accuracy, tolerance awareness, surface finishing and tool control. [PPT s.2, s.7]
- [FIT-F005] Fitting operations: marking, sawing, chipping, filing, scraping, drilling, tapping and dieing (external threading). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F006] Fitting is applied in aerospace, automotive, tool design and defence, e.g. manual fitting of turbine blades and repair of shafts and gear housings. [PPT s.11]

## 2. Fits
- [FIT-F007] There are mainly three types of fit: clearance, interference and transition. [PPT s.10]
- [FIT-F008] Clearance fit: the shaft is always smaller than the hole, giving free movement (e.g. a shaft rotating freely in a bearing). [PPT s.10]
- [FIT-F009] Interference fit: the shaft is slightly larger than the hole, so force (pressing) is needed to assemble (e.g. a gear pressed onto a shaft). [PPT s.10]
- [FIT-F010] Transition fit: the tolerance zones overlap, so there may be slight clearance or slight interference (a light push fit). [PPT s.10]

## 3. The ME-106 fitting tasks and material
- [FIT-F011] Material for the fitting job: mild-steel plate (as per the workshop drawing). [PPT s.8]
- [FIT-F012] Fitting task 1: file a metal block to exact dimensions within ±0.1 mm — to understand sawing and filing. [PPT s.3]
- [FIT-F013] Fitting task 2: create a square/triangle shape — accuracy with files. [PPT s.3]
- [FIT-F014] Fitting task 3: drill and tap a hole — to prepare for assembly. [PPT s.3]
- [FIT-F015] Fitting task 4: assemble and inspect the fit — the final project. [PPT s.3]
- [FIT-F016] Objective of the fitting lab: learn manual accuracy and verify dimensions with measuring tools. [PPT s.3]

## 4. Tools and equipment (lab list)
- [FIT-F017] Tools listed in the fitting lecture: hacksaw; flat, round, half-round, triangular and square files; hammer; try square; centre punch; Vernier caliper; bench vice (holding device). [PPT s.8]
- [FIT-F018] Bench vice — holds the workpiece firmly. [PPT s.9]
- [FIT-F019] Hacksaw — used for (rough) cutting of metal. [PPT s.9]
- [FIT-F020] Files — used for smooth finishing and shaping. [PPT s.9]
- [FIT-F021] Try square — checks/ensures 90° accuracy (squareness) of edges and surfaces. [PPT s.9]
- [FIT-F022] Centre punch — used in layout and marking, to mark drill points. [PPT s.9, s.12]
- [FIT-F023] Hammer — used for chipping and shaping (and striking punches/chisels). [PPT s.9]
- [FIT-F024] Vernier caliper / steel rule — used for precise measurement. [PPT s.9]

## 5. Holding tools
- [FIT-F026] Bench vice parts: fixed (stationary) jaw, movable (sliding) jaw, jaw plates, screw, handle, body/slide and base. [PPT s.8 (labelled vice image); theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F027] The bench vice jaws are fitted with hardened, serrated jaw plates; soft (copper/aluminium) jaw caps protect finished surfaces. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F028] The job should be held in the vice with the part to be filed or sawn projecting only a little above the jaws, to avoid vibration. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F029] A V-block has V-grooves and is used to hold round bars while marking or drilling. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F030] A C-clamp holds work to a table or angle plate; a pin vice holds small round jobs/drills; a hand vice holds small jobs in the hand. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F031] An angle plate is an L-shaped (90°) plate used to hold work perpendicular to the surface plate while marking or machining. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 6. Marking tools
- [FIT-F032] A surface plate provides a true flat reference surface for marking out and checking flatness; it is made of cast iron (or granite/hardened steel). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F033] A scriber is a hardened steel pointed tool used to scratch (scribe) lines on metal. [PPT s.12; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F034] The marking block (surface gauge / scribing block) carries a scriber on an adjustable pillar to draw lines parallel to the surface plate at a set height. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F035] An odd-leg (jenny) caliper has one bent leg and one pointed leg and is used to draw lines parallel to an edge or to find the centre of a bar. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F036] A divider (with two pointed legs) is used to draw circles and arcs and to transfer distances. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F037] A dot punch has a point angle of about 60° and makes light witness marks along scribed lines. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F038] A centre punch has a point angle of about 90° and makes a deeper mark to locate the drill point, so the drill does not slip (wander). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F039] A Vernier height gauge is used for precise marking and height measurement from the surface plate. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F040] Before scribing, the surface is often coated with chalk or marking blue so lines show clearly. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 7. Measuring tools
- [FIT-F041] The steel rule (scale) measures length to about 0.5 mm. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F042] The least count of a typical Vernier caliper is 0.02 mm. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F043] Vernier least count = value of one main-scale division − value of one Vernier-scale division (= 1 MSD / number of Vernier divisions). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F044] Vernier caliper reading = main-scale reading + (coinciding Vernier division × least count). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F045] Parts of a Vernier caliper: main scale, Vernier (sliding) scale, fixed and movable outside jaws, inside jaws, depth rod (blade), locking screw. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F046] A Vernier caliper can measure outside dimensions, inside dimensions and depth. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F047] The least count of a metric outside micrometer is 0.01 mm (pitch 0.5 mm, 50 thimble divisions). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F048] Micrometer reading = sleeve (main) reading + (thimble division × 0.01 mm). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F049] Parts of a micrometer: frame, anvil, spindle, sleeve (barrel), thimble, ratchet stop, lock nut. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F050] The ratchet of a micrometer ensures a uniform measuring pressure. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F051] Outside and inside calipers transfer dimensions and are read against a steel rule. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F052] Zero error: if the Vernier zero does not coincide with the main-scale zero when the jaws are closed, the error is subtracted (positive error) or added (negative error) to get the correct reading. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 8. Cutting tools
- [FIT-F053] A hacksaw has a frame (fixed or adjustable), a handle, a blade, and a wing nut for blade tension. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F054] The hacksaw blade is fitted with its teeth pointing forward (away from the handle), because it cuts on the forward stroke. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F055] While sawing, pressure is applied only on the forward stroke; it is released on the return stroke. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F056] Hacksaw blades are usually 250–300 mm long; finer pitch (more teeth per inch) is used for thin or hard sections and coarser pitch for soft, thick sections. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F057] Hacksaw blades are made of high-carbon steel or HSS. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F058] Parts of a file: tang, heel, face, edge, point (tip). The tang fits into the wooden handle. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F059] Files are classified by length, grade (cut), cross-section (shape) and type of cut (single or double cut). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F060] File grades from coarsest to finest: rough, bastard, second cut, smooth, dead smooth. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F061] A bastard file is used for fast removal (rough work); a smooth or dead-smooth file for final finishing. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F062] File shapes: flat, square, round, half-round, triangular (three-square) and knife-edge. [PPT s.8; theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F063] A flat file is used for flat surfaces; round and half-round files for curved surfaces and holes; triangular files for corners and angles < 90°; square files for square slots. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F064] Files are made of high-carbon steel, hardened and tempered. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F065] Cross filing is filing across the work in straight strokes; draw filing pushes and pulls the file sideways along the work for a fine finish. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F066] A file cleaner (file card) removes filings that clog the teeth (pinning). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F067] The flat cold chisel is used for chipping (cutting) metal by striking with a hammer; its cutting angle is about 60° for mild steel. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F068] Taps cut internal threads; a set of taper, intermediate (second/plug) and bottoming taps is used in that order. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F069] A tap wrench turns the tap; a die (in a die stock) cuts external threads. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F070] Tap drill size ≈ major diameter − pitch (e.g. M10 × 1.5 → 8.5 mm drill). [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F071] A twist drill (HSS) drills holes, usually on a pillar/bench drilling machine; the hole is first centre-punched. [theory: Hajra Choudhury; PPT s.12]

## 9. Striking tools
- [FIT-F072] The ball-peen hammer is the hammer most used in fitting; it has a flat face and a hemispherical (ball) peen used for riveting and peening. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F073] The cross-peen hammer has a wedge-shaped peen at right angles to the handle; the straight-peen hammer has the peen parallel to the handle. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F074] Fitting hammers are specified by weight of the head (e.g. 0.25–1 kg) and are made of forged carbon steel. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]

## 10. Procedure (our lab)
- [FIT-F075] Step 1 — Safety check: wear apron and goggles, check tools, clamp the workpiece firmly in the bench vice. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F076] Step 2 — Marking: mark dimensions with steel rule and scriber; use the try square to keep edges at 90°. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F077] Step 3 — Centre punching: light hammer blows make punch marks at drill points, to prevent the drill from slipping. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F078] Step 4 — Cutting: cut along the marked lines with a hacksaw, applying pressure only on the forward stroke. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F079] Step 5 — Filing: flat file for flat surfaces, round/half-round files for curves; keep a uniform motion. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F080] Step 6 — Checking: measure with Vernier caliper and try square; re-file if necessary. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F081] Step 7 — Fitment and inspection: assemble, check the fit and submit to the instructor. [PPT s.12]
- [FIT-F082] Correct order of fitting procedure: safety check → marking → centre punching → sawing → filing → checking → fitment & inspection. [PPT s.12]

## 11. Precautions (fitting shop)
- [FIT-F083] Always wear safety goggles, an apron and closed shoes. [PPT s.13]
- [FIT-F084] Keep the bench vice jaws tight and parallel; clamp the job securely. [PPT s.13]
- [FIT-F085] Do not use damaged tools; keep tools sharp and properly gripped. [PPT s.13]
- [FIT-F086] Do not apply excessive force while filing or sawing. [PPT s.13]
- [FIT-F087] Keep the workspace clean and safe. [PPT s.13]
- [FIT-F088] Never use a file without a handle — the tang can pierce the hand. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F089] Do not blow away or brush filings with bare hands; use a brush. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F090] Use the full length of the hacksaw blade with steady strokes and correct tension; ease the pressure near the end of the cut. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F091] Do not use a hammer with a loose head or a chisel with a mushroomed head. [theory: Hajra Choudhury]
- [FIT-F092] Safety = Skill + Awareness + Discipline (fitting-lab motto). [PPT s.13]
